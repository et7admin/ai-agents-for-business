# SAL-SOP-006 Closed-won handoff to delivery

## Purpose
Make sure the people who deliver know exactly what was sold, why the customer bought, and what was promised, so the customer never has to repeat themselves and nothing promised gets lost. A bad handoff is the most common cause of early churn.

## Scope
**In:** every closed-won deal, new or expansion.
**Out:** the onboarding itself (customer success / operations SOPs), invoicing (finance).

## Trigger
Signed paperwork is received. Complete the handoff within **one business day** ([SAL-KPI-014](https://github.com/et7admin/ai-agents-for-business/blob/main/manual/sales/kpis.md#sal-kpi-014)).

## Procedure

| # | Step | Role | Autonomy | Output |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Check the signed paperwork matches the approved proposal: price, scope, terms, dates, signatory | account-executive | A0 | Match confirmed or mismatch flagged |
| 2 | Mark the opportunity closed won with the final amount, term, start date and products | account-executive | A2 | CRM updated |
| 3 | Build the handoff package using the checklist below | account-executive | A1 | Handoff package |
| 4 | Send billing details to finance: legal entity, billing contact, amounts, schedule, payment terms, PO number | account-executive | A2 | Billing request sent |
| 5 | Hold a 30-minute internal handoff meeting with the customer-success-manager or delivery lead | account-executive | A0 | Questions answered |
| 6 | Delivery confirms it has everything it needs | customer-success-manager | A0 | Confirmation recorded |
| 7 | Introduce the customer to their delivery contact by email, restating their goals | account-executive | A2 | Introduction sent |
| 8 | Thank the customer and explain what happens next and when | account-executive | A2 | Welcome sent |
| 9 | Record the handoff completion date | account-executive | A3 | Completion logged |

## Handoff package checklist
- [ ] **Why they bought:** the problem, its impact and the goals, in the customer's words
- [ ] **Success criteria:** how the customer will judge success, with any numbers agreed
- [ ] **What was sold:** products and services, quantities, scope, exclusions
- [ ] **What was promised:** every commitment, including verbal ones and anything special agreed in negotiation
- [ ] **Stakeholders:** economic buyer, champion, day-to-day contact, technical contacts, with roles and how each likes to communicate
- [ ] **Timeline:** start date, milestones, deadlines that matter to the customer and why
- [ ] **Commercial terms:** contract value, term, renewal date, payment terms, special conditions
- [ ] **Risks and sensitivities:** concerns raised during the sale, internal politics, past bad experiences, competitors they left
- [ ] **Documents:** links to the signed contract, final proposal, discovery summaries

## Decision rules
- If the paperwork doesn't match the approved proposal (step 1), stop. Resolve it with the sales-manager before marking closed won.
- If something was promised that delivery can't meet, raise it in the handoff meeting and agree with the customer how to handle it. Never hide it.
- The account-executive stays reachable to delivery for the first 30 days for questions about the sale.

## Quality checks
- [ ] Every checklist item is filled in or marked "not applicable" with a reason.
- [ ] Delivery confirmed receipt (step 6). Sales saying it's done isn't enough.
- [ ] The customer got the introduction and welcome within one business day of signing.
- [ ] Finance received billing details.

## Exceptions & escalation
| Situation | What to do | Escalate to |
|---|---|---|
| Paperwork differs from the approved proposal | Stop; don't mark closed won | sales-manager |
| Delivery has no capacity for the agreed start date | Agree a plan before telling the customer | head-of-operations |
| Customer requests a change right after signing | Log it; route as a change request, not a sales concession | sales-manager |

## Records
Handoff package, meeting notes, delivery confirmation, emails, completion date. Kept with the account in the CRM.
